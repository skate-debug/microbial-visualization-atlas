# Environmental microbiology visualization catalogue

116 formats and applied recipes in 16 families. Literature checked on 2026-10-27.

This is a broad, curated planning resource covering soil, freshwater, marine, sediment, subsurface, engineered and host-associated environmental systems. It includes standard encodings and useful domain-specific compositions; the entries are not all mathematically distinct chart types. It is not a systematic review or an exhaustive inventory of figures in Nature, Science and Cell.

Choose a scientific question and a valid analytical unit before choosing visual styling. Each display depends on appropriate analysis of the study data.

**Evidence key:** D = encoding verified in a cited figure or original method; A = an adaptation inspired by the cited study; P = additional proposal without a claimed inspected journal example. D confirms the encoding, not every suggested application or design refinement. Nature Portfolio journals are identified by their actual titles in the [journal atlas](journal-atlas.md). Host-associated and cultured-cell method transfers are labelled there.

Evidence coverage: 43 direct, 20 adaptations, 53 additional proposals. Primary study examples and supplementary method references are distinguished in the reference atlas.

The **starter** tag means broadly useful for an initial implementation; **specialist** means useful when the corresponding data and scientific question are available. Neither is a quality ranking.

## Sampling, diversity and distributions

| ID / format | Scientific use and minimum data | Design and interpretation | Evidence / scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **DIV01 · Boxplot with observations**<br>starter | How does alpha diversity vary among habitats?<br>**Data:** Per-sample metric, group and biological replicate. | Overlay restrained raw points and show group sample sizes.<br>**Limit:** Define whiskers; many technical replicates do not increase biological n. | D: [R06](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-020-0718-6) Fig. 1b |
| **DIV02 · Violin or bean plot**<br>specialist | Are distributions skewed or multimodal?<br>**Data:** Enough independent observations per group. | Align density shapes and add median and observations.<br>**Limit:** Kernel bandwidth can invent apparent modes in small samples. | D: [R02](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359) Fig. 4b and Fig. 8b |
| **DIV03 · Raincloud plot**<br>starter | How do distribution, observations and central tendency agree?<br>**Data:** Per-unit values and group IDs. | Combine half-density, points and a compact interval summary.<br>**Limit:** Density is unreliable with very small n; specify the interval meaning. | D: [M04](https://doi.org/10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15191.1) Method examples |
| **DIV04 · Ridgeline distributions**<br>specialist | How do distributions shift across many ordered groups?<br>**Data:** Repeated comparable measurements and an ordered grouping. | Use a common axis and modest overlap.<br>**Limit:** Per-ridge normalization hides differences in sample quantity. | P |
| **DIV05 · Empirical cumulative distribution function**<br>starter | What fraction of samples lies below a threshold?<br>**Data:** Individual values, including eligible zeros. | Label selected thresholds and use direct group labels.<br>**Limit:** Distinguish censoring and missing data from measured zeros. | D: [R13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01686-x) Fig. 3a |
| **DIV06 · Histogram or density overlay**<br>specialist | What is the size or spread of a measurement?<br>**Data:** Contig lengths, cell sizes or other per-unit values. | Use common bins and a clear log scale when needed.<br>**Limit:** Conclusions should not depend on a convenient bin width. | D: [R03](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.03.040) Fig. 1b |
| **DIV07 · Rank-abundance curve**<br>starter | Is a community dominated by a few populations?<br>**Data:** Comparable abundance estimates and taxon ranks. | Emphasize dominance and tail shape with consistent axes.<br>**Limit:** Relative and absolute abundance answer different questions. | D: [R08](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13219) Fig. 3 |
| **DIV08 · Rarefaction or accumulation curve**<br>starter | How much additional diversity does sampling reveal?<br>**Data:** Count data or discovery histories, effort and resampling results. | Show observed effort, uncertainty and any extrapolation boundary.<br>**Limit:** Distinguish sample-based from read-based effort; do not equate saturation with complete sampling. | D: [R02](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359) Fig. 1c; [R13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01686-x) Extended Data Fig. 1 |
| **DIV09 · Hill-diversity profile**<br>specialist | Do group rankings depend on sensitivity to rare taxa?<br>**Data:** Diversity estimates across orders q with uncertainty. | Plot effective diversity on shared axes across q.<br>**Limit:** Richness, Shannon-derived and Simpson-derived measures need compatible estimation. | P |

| **COM03 · Abundance–prevalence dot matrix**<br>starter | Which taxa are abundant and consistently detected?<br>**Data:** Group-level abundance and detection fraction from sample-level data. | Use dot area for prevalence and colour for abundance.<br>**Limit:** Area must encode magnitude; disclose aggregation and detection thresholds. | P |
| **COM04 · Ordered presence–absence matrix**<br>starter | Are communities nested or characterized by turnover?<br>**Data:** Binary occurrence matrix and detection definition. | Order rows and columns by a justified ecological criterion.<br>**Limit:** Non-detection is not proof of absence; ordering affects apparent nestedness. | D: [R01](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24621) Fig. 3a–b |
| **COM05 · Ternary composition plot**<br>specialist | How do samples divide among three components?<br>**Data:** Three mutually exclusive components summing to a common whole. | Use readable corner labels and restrained reference lines.<br>**Limit:** Other components cannot disappear silently; closure induces dependence. | P |
| **COM06 · Taxonomic treemap**<br>specialist | How is abundance distributed within a hierarchy?<br>**Data:** Consistent taxonomy and nested totals. | Limit hierarchy depth and label major regions.<br>**Limit:** Area is weak for precise comparisons; parent and child totals must reconcile. | P |
| **COM07 · Taxonomic sunburst or icicle plot**<br>specialist | How do broad groups subdivide into finer taxa?<br>**Data:** A genuine taxonomy with counts at leaves. | Prefer an interactive drill-down with an accessible tabular counterpart.<br>**Limit:** Equal-looking sectors can be hard to compare; distinguish taxonomy from phylogeny. | P |
| **COM08 · UpSet intersection plot**<br>starter | Which genes or taxa are shared among many groups?<br>**Data:** Explicit sets with unique entity IDs. | Align the membership matrix with intersection and set sizes.<br>**Limit:** Specify exclusive versus inclusive intersections and account for filtered entities. | D: [M01](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6876017/) Method examples |
| **COM09 · Venn or Euler diagram**<br>specialist | Which members are shared among two or three small sets?<br>**Data:** Set memberships and intersection counts. | Use a few labelled sets; prioritize numbers over decorative areas.<br>**Limit:** More than a few sets becomes unreadable; area proportionality is not automatic. | D: [R03](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.03.040) Fig. 6a (larger-set Venn; simpler use recommended here) |
| **COM10 · Membership alluvial diagram**<br>specialist | How do classifications or memberships connect across levels?<br>**Data:** Entity-level links with counts or weights. | Minimize crossings and define ribbon width.<br>**Limit:** This represents membership, not necessarily temporal movement or conserved material. | P |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **ORD02 · PCA biplot**<br>specialist | Which measured variables explain sample variation?<br>**Data:** Appropriately transformed continuous matrix and loadings. | Show only interpretable loading vectors with the scaling stated.<br>**Limit:** PCA on untransformed relative abundance can be misleading. | P |
| **ORD03 · Constrained ordination or environmental-vector overlay**<br>starter | How does community structure relate to measured gradients?<br>**Data:** RDA/CCA/dbRDA scores or fitted vectors on an ordination. | Reduce overlapping arrows and show the chosen scaling.<br>**Limit:** A constrained analysis differs from post-hoc vector fitting; neither establishes causation. | A: [R04](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0386-6) Extended Data Fig. 5i–l verifies the vector-overlay variant |
| **ORD04 · Chronological ordination trajectory**<br>starter | How does a community move after a disturbance?<br>**Data:** One shared ordination, timestamps and replicate IDs. | Connect observations within each real time series only.<br>**Limit:** Axis drift across separately fitted ordinations invalidates apparent trajectories. | P |

| **ORD06 · Distance-decay display**<br>starter | Does community dissimilarity rise with geographic or environmental distance?<br>**Data:** Pairwise distances and identities of the underlying samples. | Show data density or distance-bin distributions with a justified fit.<br>**Limit:** Pairwise comparisons share samples and are not independent replicates. | D: [R02](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359) Fig. 4b |
| **ORD07 · Joint scatterplot with marginal distributions**<br>starter | How do macrodiversity and microdiversity vary together?<br>**Data:** Two per-sample metrics and group labels. | Align marginal distributions with the central scatter axes.<br>**Limit:** Aggregated group relationships can differ from sample-level relationships. | D: [R03](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.03.040) Fig. 4b |

## Effects, differential abundance and associations

| ID / format | Scientific use and minimum data | Design and interpretation | Evidence / scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EFF01 · Forest or coefficient interval plot**<br>starter | How large and uncertain are treatment effects?<br>**Data:** Effect estimates, intervals, units and reference groups. | Align estimates to a meaningful null line and order scientifically.<br>**Limit:** State the model and interval type; effect estimates must respect the study design. | P |
| **EFF02 · Volcano plot**<br>specialist | Which features show substantial and supported changes?<br>**Data:** Valid effect sizes and adjusted significance values. | Label a small, pre-specified subset and disclose thresholds.<br>**Limit:** A visually extreme point may have unstable low counts; use composition-aware inference where needed. | P |
| **EFF03 · MA or mean–difference plot**<br>specialist | Does apparent change depend on feature abundance?<br>**Data:** Mean abundance and estimated between-group change. | Use density shading for crowds and highlight robust features.<br>**Limit:** State the transformation; pseudocounts can dominate rare-feature effects. | P |
| **EFF04 · Paired slope or dumbbell plot**<br>starter | Do the same samples or sites change under treatment?<br>**Data:** Paired observations and stable pair IDs. | Keep pair connections light and emphasize a summary only if useful.<br>**Limit:** Pairing cannot be inferred from two unrelated groups. | P |
| **EFF05 · Modelled response curve with uncertainty**<br>starter | Is a response linear, saturating or unimodal?<br>**Data:** Raw observations, model predictions and uncertainty. | Keep observations visible and show the sampled predictor range.<br>**Limit:** Smooth curves are model outputs; avoid unsupported extrapolation. | A: [R04](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0386-6) Fig. 1 verifies fitted diversity–latitude curves |
| **EFF06 · Interaction or reaction-norm plot**<br>specialist | Does a treatment effect depend on an environmental context?<br>**Data:** Factorial or longitudinal estimates with appropriate uncertainty. | Use shared axes and a concise line-style key.<br>**Limit:** Interactions need estimable contrasts and sufficient replication. | P |
| **EFF07 · Correlation heatmap or correlogram**<br>starter | Which environmental measurements covary?<br>**Data:** Pairwise association estimates and sample availability. | Use a diverging scale centered on zero and meaningful ordering.<br>**Limit:** Correlation is not causation; composition, multiple testing and repeated observations matter. | D: [R04](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0386-6) Fig. 2a |
| **EFF08 · Environmental association matrix with linked edges**<br>specialist | Which community summaries associate with environmental variables?<br>**Data:** Environmental correlations and justified community-distance association estimates. | Combine a compact matrix with a small number of clearly keyed links.<br>**Limit:** Partial Mantel-style displays are not universal causal or confounder-control solutions. | D: [R02](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359) Fig. 5b |

## Time series and disturbance dynamics

| ID / format | Scientific use and minimum data | Design and interpretation | Evidence / scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **TIM01 · Aligned abundance and environment time series**<br>starter | Do community changes coincide with environmental events?<br>**Data:** Time-stamped measurements with replicate identities. | Align time axes; show raw observations alongside any smoothing.<br>**Limit:** Do not interpolate long gaps or confuse temporal association with a mechanism. | D: [R10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02571-4) Fig. 2 and Fig. 3a–b |
| **TIM02 · Time-ordered taxon heatmap**<br>starter | Which taxa bloom or disappear together?<br>**Data:** Taxon-by-time matrix and sampling intervals. | Preserve chronology and add event or chemistry tracks.<br>**Limit:** Row scaling and uneven intervals can exaggerate apparent synchrony. | A: [R10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02571-4) Fig. 1 and Fig. 3 temporal structure |
| **TIM03 · Stacked-area succession plot**<br>specialist | How does overall composition change over time?<br>**Data:** Comparable relative or absolute abundances through time. | Use stable taxon order and directly label dominant components.<br>**Limit:** Interpolation implies continuity; changing relative areas need not reflect biomass changes. | P |
| **TIM04 · Time–depth heatmap or Hovmöller diagram**<br>specialist | When do microbial or chemical layers shift?<br>**Data:** Repeated depth profiles with timestamps. | Overlay measured sampling support and align related variables.<br>**Limit:** Sparse sampling does not support smooth migrating fronts without a model. | P |
| **TIM05 · Lagged association correlogram**<br>specialist | Does one process tend to precede another?<br>**Data:** Sufficiently long aligned time series and lag estimates. | Plot signed lag with uncertainty or null-reference bands.<br>**Limit:** Autocorrelation, uneven sampling and shared drivers can generate false lead–lag patterns. | P |
| **TIM06 · Wavelet or time–frequency spectrum**<br>specialist | Does periodicity vary during a bloom or season?<br>**Data:** A sufficiently resolved time series and a wavelet analysis. | Label periods and the cone of influence clearly.<br>**Limit:** Edge effects and temporal resolution constrain interpretable cycles. | A: [R10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02571-4) Wavelet-based analysis; proposed spectrum display |
| **TIM07 · Disturbance–recovery trajectory**<br>starter | How resistant and resilient are communities?<br>**Data:** Before/during/after measurements, controls and a defined reference state. | Align the perturbation window and show replicate paths.<br>**Limit:** Return in one metric does not prove complete ecological recovery. | P |

## Ecological networks and relational structure

| **NET01 · Module-focused node–link network**<br>starter | Which association modules organize an ecological dataset?<br>**Data:** Nodes, filtered edges, signs, weights and inference method. | Use a fixed layout seed and clear module labels.<br>**Limit:** Co-occurrence does not prove interaction; compositional and environmental confounding matter. | D: [R05](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1262073) Fig. 3b; [R14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02347-2) Fig. 4a |
| **NET02 · Adjacency matrix**<br>starter | Where are dense, signed or cross-module associations?<br>**Data:** A defined adjacency matrix and node metadata. | Order by modules and use a centered signed scale.<br>**Limit:** Make filtered, untested and zero-valued edges distinguishable. | P |
| **NET03 · Chord diagram**<br>specialist | Which higher-level groups have strong many-to-many links?<br>**Data:** Aggregated links with a declared weight and denominator. | Limit sectors and separate positive from negative associations.<br>**Limit:** Ribbons encode association totals, not material exchange; aggregation can conceal heterogeneity. | D: [R05](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1262073) Fig. 2a |
| **NET04 · Ego-network or focal subnetwork**<br>specialist | What connects a focal taxon, metabolite or process?<br>**Data:** Full network and explicit focal-node selection rule. | Keep neighbours readable and summarize the surrounding context.<br>**Limit:** A visually central node is not necessarily an ecological keystone. | P |
| **NET05 · Comparable network small multiples**<br>specialist | How do relational patterns differ across biomes or treatments?<br>**Data:** Comparable network estimates and stable node identities. | Reuse node positions, filters and edge scaling.<br>**Limit:** Unequal sample sizes or thresholds can manufacture differences in topology. | D: [R05](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1262073) Fig. 2d–g; [R14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02347-2) Fig. 4a |
| **NET06 · Network statistic versus null-model display**<br>specialist | Are connectivity or modularity patterns unusual under a stated null?<br>**Data:** Observed statistics, null ensembles and uncertainty. | Show reference distributions alongside observed values.<br>**Limit:** A result depends on the null model; graph fit does not validate biological mechanisms. | A: [R14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02347-2) Fig. 4b degree-distribution comparisons |

## Function, metabolism and biogeochemistry

| ID / format | Scientific use and minimum data | Design and interpretation | Evidence / scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FUN01 · Pathway completeness heatmap**<br>starter | Which genomes encode which metabolic capacities?<br>**Data:** Genome-by-pathway calls, evidence and completeness metadata. | Group pathways by process and align genome-quality tracks.<br>**Limit:** MAG incompleteness and alternative pathway routes complicate absence calls. | P |
| **FUN02 · Functional enrichment dot plot**<br>starter | Which processes are overrepresented among selected features?<br>**Data:** Enrichment statistics, term sizes, background universe and adjusted values. | Use dot area for a meaningful quantity and colour for one statistic.<br>**Limit:** The tested background and overlapping terms affect interpretation. | P |
| **FUN03 · Enrichment similarity network**<br>specialist | Which enriched pathways share genes?<br>**Data:** Gene-set membership and a specified overlap measure. | Collapse redundant terms and label coherent groups.<br>**Limit:** Links represent shared membership, not biochemical reactions. | P |
| **FUN04 · Annotated biogeochemical-cycle map**<br>starter | Which organisms may perform steps in carbon, nitrogen or sulfur cycling?<br>**Data:** Supported reactions with genes, taxa and abundance annotations. | Use recognizable chemical species and a compact directional layout.<br>**Limit:** Genome-derived potential is not measured activity or flux. | D: [R08](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13219) Fig. 4; [R09](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19366) Fig. 3a–b |
| **FUN05 · Measured-flux Sankey diagram**<br>specialist | How does quantified material move between compartments?<br>**Data:** Comparable, mass-balanced flux estimates and units. | Make arrow width proportional to flux and show residual pools.<br>**Limit:** Gene counts and relative abundance cannot be substituted for material flux. | P |
| **FUN06 · Reaction-level pathway map**<br>starter | Which enzymes or metabolites differ along a pathway?<br>**Data:** Reaction graph with measurements mapped to stable identifiers. | Use a restrained diverging scale and separate unmeasured reactions.<br>**Limit:** Distinguish annotation, expression, metabolite concentration and estimated flux. | D: [R13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01686-x) Fig. 4 and Extended Data pathway figures |
| **FUN07 · Isotope or stoichiometric relationship plot**<br>specialist | Do isotope signatures or elemental ratios distinguish processes?<br>**Data:** Measured isotope/element values, units, uncertainty and reference endmembers. | Use clear bivariate axes or an appropriately defined ternary system.<br>**Limit:** Mixing lines and source fractions require justified endmembers and models. | P |
| **FUN08 · Metabolic-handoff schematic**<br>specialist | Which organisms could exchange sequential metabolic products?<br>**Data:** Evidence-linked reaction capabilities and proposed exchange paths. | Distinguish measured exchange from hypothesized links graphically.<br>**Limit:** Shared capabilities alone do not establish metabolite transfer. | D: [R08](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13219) Fig. 6 |

| **IMG06 · Raman or NanoSIMS activity map**<br>specialist | Which cells or microregions incorporate labelled substrates?<br>**Data:** Measured spectra or ion maps, controls and spatial registration. | Align activity signal with cell boundaries or taxon labels.<br>**Limit:** Isotope enrichment and spectral peaks need calibrated biological interpretation. | P |

## Prediction and machine-learning assessment

| ID / format | Scientific use and minimum data | Design and interpretation | Evidence / scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ML01 · Observed-versus-predicted scatterplot**<br>starter | Does a model predict environmental measurements accurately?<br>**Data:** Held-out predictions, observations and split labels. | Include the identity line and meaningful error summaries.<br>**Limit:** Training fit is not generalization; spatial or site leakage can inflate performance. | D: [R02](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359) Fig. 6b |
| **ML02 · ROC and precision–recall curves**<br>starter | How well does a classifier separate classes?<br>**Data:** Held-out scores, labels and class prevalence. | Pair curves with prevalence-aware baselines and uncertainty.<br>**Limit:** ROC can look strong for rare classes while precision remains poor. | A: [R01](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24621) Extended Data Fig. 5d verifies ROC; PR is an additional companion |
| **ML03 · Confusion matrix**<br>starter | Which habitats or classes are confused?<br>**Data:** Held-out labels, predictions and class counts. | Show raw support and clearly state normalization direction.<br>**Limit:** Row-normalized percentages can hide poorly represented classes. | D: [R01](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24621) Extended Data Fig. 5c |
| **ML04 · Calibration or reliability plot**<br>specialist | Do predicted probabilities match observed frequencies?<br>**Data:** Held-out probabilities and outcomes. | Add probability-bin support and uncertainty.<br>**Limit:** Calibration depends on prevalence and binning; discrimination and calibration differ. | P |
| **ML05 · Residual diagnostic panels**<br>starter | Where does a model systematically fail?<br>**Data:** Held-out residuals, predictions, location and covariates. | Align residual-versus-fit, covariate and spatial views as needed.<br>**Limit:** Spatially structured residuals invalidate claims of uniformly good performance. | P |
| **ML06 · Permutation-importance interval plot**<br>specialist | Which features support predictive performance?<br>**Data:** Repeated held-out importance estimates and a defined metric. | Show uncertainty and group correlated predictors when justified.<br>**Limit:** Importance is model-dependent and not a causal effect; correlated predictors share signal. | A: [R04](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0386-6) Extended Data Fig. 4 verifies variable-importance encoding |
| **ML07 · SHAP beeswarm or dependence plot**<br>specialist | How does a fitted model allocate prediction contributions?<br>**Data:** Valid explanation values, input features and background specification. | Label units, reference output and a small set of important features.<br>**Limit:** SHAP attribution is not causality and depends on assumptions about dependent features. | P |
| **ML08 · Validation-stratum performance plot**<br>starter | Does performance transfer across sites, seasons or biomes?<br>**Data:** Per-stratum held-out metrics, uncertainty and sample counts. | Align interval estimates and expose low-support strata.<br>**Limit:** Keep tuning outside the test sets; overall performance can hide subgroup failure. | P |

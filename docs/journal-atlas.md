# Journal and figure reference atlas

References were selected for environmental relevance, range of encodings, traceable figure locations and reusable design ideas. This is a curated source set, not a ranking of journals or a frequency analysis of their figures. Publication in a high-profile journal does not make every design choice optimal.

Verification uses primary articles, their publicly accessible manuscripts and figure captions. Four layouts were also inspected directly: Gregory Fig. 4, Sunagawa Figs. 4–5, Lima-Mendez Fig. 2 and Bahram Fig. 1. Other examples were checked at the caption or article level; no claim of visual inspection of every panel is made. Public manuscript versions are identified below where relevant.

## Environmental and microbiological study references

### R01 · Thompson et al. (2017)

[A communal catalogue reveals Earth’s multiscale microbial diversity](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24621). *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/nature24621.

**Scope:** Flagship; environmental microbiology.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1b: sampling map; Fig. 2: diversity and ordination; Fig. 3a–b: ordered presence–absence matrices; Extended Data Fig. 3b: Procrustes comparison; Extended Data Fig. 5c–f: classification and source tracking.

**Design lesson:** Use consistent environmental categories to connect geographic, diversity and community views.

**Checked:** Publisher-indexed figure captions.

### R02 · Sunagawa et al. (2015)

[Structure and function of the global ocean microbiome](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261359). *Science*. DOI: 10.1126/science.1261359.

**Scope:** Flagship; marine microbiology.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1a,c: map and gene accumulation; Fig. 3: ordination and depth comparisons; Fig. 4b: distance decay; Fig. 5b: environmental correlation matrix linked to community-distance associations; Fig. 6b: observed versus predicted temperature; Fig. 8a: aligned taxonomic and functional profiles.

**Design lesson:** Pair community patterns with environmental gradients and prediction checks.

**Checked:** Primary article PDF and captions; Fig. 4–5 layouts visually inspected.

[Public article or manuscript](https://fondationtaraocean.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Science-2015-Sunagawa-.pdf).

### R03 · Gregory et al. (2019)

[Marine DNA Viral Macro- and Microdiversity from Pole to Pole](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.03.040). *Cell*. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2019.03.040.

**Scope:** Flagship; marine viromics.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1a–b: sampling map and length histograms; Fig. 2a: read-recruitment profiles; Fig. 3: ecological-scale schematic; Fig. 4a–b: PCoA and scatterplot with marginal boxplots; Fig. 6a: set overlap; Fig. 7: geographic and depth gradients.

**Design lesson:** Combine multivariate structure, marginal distributions and ecological context in one coordinated figure.

**Checked:** Primary article PDF and captions; Fig. 4 layout visually inspected. Public PDF is an article-in-press version.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6525058/).

### R04 · Bahram et al. (2018)

[Structure and function of the global topsoil microbiome](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0386-6). *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-018-0386-6.

**Scope:** Flagship; soil bacteria and fungi.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1: matched diversity–latitude panels; Fig. 2a,c: correlation matrix and structural equation model; Extended Data Fig. 4: variable-importance/correlation dot matrices; Extended Data Fig. 5i–l: NMDS with environmental vectors.

**Design lesson:** Keep bacterial and fungal comparisons aligned while showing both response shape and environmental associations.

**Checked:** Publisher-indexed captions and accepted-manuscript PDF; Fig. 1 layout visually inspected.

[Public article or manuscript](https://edoc.mdc-berlin.de/id/eprint/17657/13/17657oa.pdf).

### R05 · Lima-Mendez et al. (2015)

[Determinants of community structure in the global plankton interactome](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1262073). *Science*. DOI: 10.1126/science.1262073.

**Scope:** Flagship; marine ecological associations.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 2a,d–g: association chord diagrams and geographic comparisons; Fig. 3b: aggregated network; Fig. 3e: sequence-context comparison; Fig. 4b–d: microscopy and three-dimensional reconstruction.

**Design lesson:** Connect association summaries to independent biological evidence and retain a clear definition of every edge.

**Checked:** Primary article PDF and captions; Fig. 2 layout visually inspected.

[Public article or manuscript](https://bio.kuleuven.be/highlights_eeb/metacommunity_ecology/2015-eeb-lima-mendez-et-al-science-2015.pdf).

### R06 · Nayfach et al. (2021)

[A genomic catalog of Earth’s microbiomes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-020-0718-6). *Nature Biotechnology*. DOI: 10.1038/s41587-020-0718-6.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; environmental genome catalogues.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1b: genome-quality distributions; Fig. 3a: phylogeny with concentric metadata tracks; Fig. 4b: biosynthetic gene-cluster architecture; Fig. 5: host-link evidence and viral phylogeny.

**Design lesson:** Use a shared tree or genomic coordinate system to align multiple layers of annotation.

**Checked:** Publisher-indexed text and primary full-text XML figure captions. Online publication was 2020; issue year is 2021.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8041624/).

### R07 · Hug et al. (2016)

[A new view of the tree of life](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmicrobiol201648). *Nature Microbiology*. DOI: 10.1038/nmicrobiol.2016.48.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; microbial phylogeny.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1: broad phylogenetic overview of the tree of life.

**Design lesson:** Use overview trees to show evolutionary coverage, with readable clade summaries and focused detail views.

**Checked:** Publisher article text; cited as a phylogenetic overview reference, without claiming a particular circular-track design.

### R08 · Anantharaman et al. (2016)

[Thousands of microbial genomes shed light on interconnected biogeochemical processes in an aquifer system](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13219). *Nature Communications*. DOI: 10.1038/ncomms13219.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; subsurface biogeochemistry.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1: sampling scheme; Fig. 2: collapsed phylogeny; Fig. 3: rank-abundance displays; Fig. 4: annotated element cycles; Fig. 6: metabolic-handoff schematic.

**Design lesson:** Connect genome-derived metabolic potential to an explicit ecological model without calling it measured flux.

**Checked:** Primary full-text XML figure captions.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5079060/).

### R09 · Roux et al. (2016)

[Ecogenomics and potential biogeochemical impacts of globally abundant ocean viruses](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19366). *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/nature19366.

**Scope:** Flagship; viral ecology and auxiliary metabolic genes.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 3a–b: pathway schematics; Extended Data Fig. 5–7: AMG phylogeny, abundance and genomic context, including P-II in Extended Data Fig. 7.

**Design lesson:** Link gene identity, genomic context, environmental distribution and metabolic interpretation.

**Checked:** Publisher-indexed captions and author-manuscript figure legends.

### R10 · Martin-Platero et al. (2018)

[High resolution time series reveals cohesive but short-lived communities in coastal plankton](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02571-4). *Nature Communications*. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-017-02571-4.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; marine time series.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1: taxonomic dynamics at different resolutions; Fig. 2: daily and smoothed trajectories; Fig. 3a–b: community dynamics aligned with environmental heatmaps.

**Design lesson:** Align biological and environmental time axes and distinguish short-term fluctuations from longer trends.

**Checked:** Publisher-indexed captions and primary full-text XML.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5773528/).

### R11 · Shi et al. (2020)

[Highly multiplexed spatial mapping of microbial communities](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2983-4). *Nature*. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-020-2983-4.

**Scope:** Flagship; host-associated spatial microbiology, transferable to environmental imaging.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 2b–g: segmented cells, pair-correlation functions and volumetric views; Fig. 3a,f: labelled images and spatial association networks; Fig. 4: oral biofilm spatial organization.

**Design lesson:** Pair cell-level images with quantitative spatial summaries; distinguish fields of view from biological replicates.

**Checked:** Primary full-text XML figure captions. Environmental applications in this catalogue are transfers from host-associated examples.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8050837/).

### R12 · Kuchina et al. (2021)

[Microbial single-cell RNA sequencing by split-pool barcoding](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aba5257). *Science*. DOI: 10.1126/science.aba5257.

**Scope:** Flagship; cultured bacterial single-cell method, transferable to environmental isolates.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1: method schematic, species-mixing quality-control scatter and t-SNE visualization.

**Design lesson:** Use embeddings to explore measured cell-state heterogeneity and show technical validation alongside them.

**Checked:** Primary article and PubMed-indexed figure captions; t-SNE verified, not UMAP. Online publication was 2020; issue year is 2021.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8269303/).

### R13 · Graham et al. (2024)

[A global atlas of soil viruses reveals unexplored biodiversity and potential biogeochemical impacts](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01686-x). *Nature Microbiology*. DOI: 10.1038/s41564-024-01686-x.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; soil viromics.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 1a: sampling map; Fig. 3a–c: cumulative distribution, host phylogeny with abundance rings and environmental correlation heatmap; Fig. 4: annotated cellular metabolic pathways; Extended Data Fig. 1: rarefaction.

**Design lesson:** Integrate geography, host evidence and functional potential with complementary encodings.

**Checked:** Primary full-text XML figure captions.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11222151/).

### R14 · Ma et al. (2024)

[Biogeographic patterns and drivers of soil viromes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-024-02347-2). *Nature Ecology & Evolution*. DOI: 10.1038/s41559-024-02347-2.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; soil viral biogeography.

**Figure locations:** Fig. 4a–b: network modules, regional/biome subnetworks and degree-distribution comparisons with model networks. The study also reports global diversity predictions.

**Design lesson:** Keep comparative network definitions and display rules consistent across regions.

**Checked:** Publisher Fig. 4 caption and primary article abstract; prediction-map recipe is labelled as an adaptation.

[Public article or manuscript](https://escholarship.org/content/qt86g5f9wh/qt86g5f9wh.pdf).

## Additional methods and design references

These provide original attribution for useful visualization methods. Their journal names are stated explicitly; they are not presented as flagship Nature, Science or Cell papers.

### M01 · Lex et al. (2014)

[UpSet: Visualization of Intersecting Sets](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6876017/). *IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics*. DOI: 10.1109/TVCG.2014.2346248.

**Scope:** Additional visualization-method source.

**Figure locations:** Set-membership matrix linked to intersection sizes and set sizes.

**Design lesson:** Make many-set intersections comparable through aligned quantitative encodings.

**Checked:** Original method paper; this is not a Nature, Science or Cell publication.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4720993/).

### M02 · Gu, Eils and Schlesner (2016)

[Complex heatmaps reveal patterns and correlations in multidimensional genomic data](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/32/18/2847/1743594). *Bioinformatics*. DOI: 10.1093/bioinformatics/btw313.

**Scope:** Additional visualization-method source.

**Figure locations:** Parallel heatmaps, annotations and shared ordering.

**Design lesson:** Use coordinated matrix tracks to integrate heterogeneous measurements.

**Checked:** Original method paper and author-maintained documentation; not a Nature, Science or Cell publication.

### M03 · Crameri, Shephard and Heron (2020)

[The misuse of colour in science communication](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19160-7). *Nature Communications*. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7.

**Scope:** Nature Portfolio; visualization-design guidance.

**Figure locations:** Scientific colour-map examples and selection guidance.

**Design lesson:** Choose perceptually appropriate, colour-vision-accessible scales.

**Checked:** Publisher article and abstract.

### M04 · Allen et al. (2019)

[Raincloud plots: a multi-platform tool for robust data visualization](https://doi.org/10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15191.1). *Wellcome Open Research*. DOI: 10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15191.1.

**Scope:** Additional visualization-method source.

**Figure locations:** Raw observations, density and summary statistics in a coordinated distribution display.

**Design lesson:** Show the data distribution and observations alongside a summary.

**Checked:** Original publication in 2019; the accessible PMC record also includes a revised 2021 version. Not a Nature, Science or Cell publication.

[Public article or manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6480976/).

### M05 · Argelaguet et al. (2020)

[MOFA+: a statistical framework for comprehensive integration of multi-modal single-cell data](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-020-02015-1). *Genome Biology*. DOI: 10.1186/s13059-020-02015-1.

**Scope:** Additional multi-omics method source.

**Figure locations:** Multi-modal latent factors and feature weights; environmental use is an adaptation.

**Design lesson:** Separate shared sample variation from the features contributing to it.

**Checked:** Original method paper; not a Nature, Science or Cell publication.

## Reuse in a GitHub project

Create original examples using your own data or appropriately licensed public data. Keep links and figure locators as design references. If you later include a journal image, record that image’s applicable licence and attribution; article accessibility alone does not identify its reuse terms. Treat figure images, geographic assets, code and data as separately credited materials.

The present repository contains original catalogue text and reference metadata, not reproduced journal figures or downloaded article PDFs.
